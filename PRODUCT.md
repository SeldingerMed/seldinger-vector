# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Engineers and research leads building medical robotics, procedural-video models, and world models. They arrive skeptical and need to determine whether Vector can run a real model against a pinned task, preserve safety outcomes as a vector, and produce an artifact another party can replay.

## Product Purpose

Vector is independent evaluation infrastructure for procedural medical AI. It binds immutable `org/name@version` task and agent packages by interface capabilities, executes agent and verifier code across explicit process boundaries, and emits replayable vector scorecards whose safety gates cannot be averaged away. Success means a technical visitor can run a closed-loop, multi-turn interactive, structured single-turn, or counterfactual evaluation; inspect the exact task, runtime, world pin, typed trace, vector, projection rule, and artifact head; and independently replay the result.

## Brand Personality

Exact, operational, and evidence-first. The voice states what ran, which interface bound, what passed, what abstained, and which artifact another team can replay.

## Anti-references

Generic AI SaaS pages, blue or teal hospital branding, stock operating-room imagery, glossy dashboards, glass effects, abstract gradients, vague “trustworthy AI” claims, inflated clinical language, and visual imitation of Harbor rather than adaptation of its restraint.

## Design Principles

1. Proof before promise: show commands, interfaces, pins, vectors, hashes, and replay paths before claims.
2. Execution before contact: the primary call to action runs the product; sales contact is secondary.
3. Preserve the vector: never make an aggregate score the dominant representation.
4. Make evaluation state explicit: interaction mode, abstentions, gate outcomes, typed metrics, and projection identity belong in the primary surface.
5. Earn technical trust: every public result links to a reproducible package or artifact.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Preserve visible keyboard focus, adequate contrast, readable command overflow at narrow widths, touch targets of at least 44 pixels, semantic landmarks and tables, and reduced-motion behavior. Never encode pass, fail, or unassessable states by color alone.
